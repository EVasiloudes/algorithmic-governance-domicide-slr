# Thematic Synthesis — Sub-theme Clusters

*Generated 2026-07-29 from `combined_coding_275.csv` justifications, theses, and key quotes (records scoring ≥2 per theme). Clusters are analytic groupings for the Results chapter; records may appear in more than one cluster (primary assignment listed first).*

## Theme 1 — Dual-use / information-good properties (n=86 scoring ≥2; 11 scoring 3)

### T1-A · Smart-city IoT as military operating environment (civil→military, affirmative/technical) — ~18 records
The largest and most surprising cluster: defence-research and technical literatures that *affirmatively design* the exploitation of civilian smart-city infrastructure for military situational awareness. Dual-use here is not a critique but a design goal.
- Exemplars: Pradhan et al. (2018, three papers — NATO data-model interoperability); Riberto et al. (2018, Helsinki Digitraffic case); Tsiolis & Leivadeas (2025, "Internet of Military Defense Things"); Johnsen et al. (2018); Suri et al. (2018); Cohen et al. (2018, COTS software-defined radio); Kunst et al. (2018)
- Grey/policy: Future Cities (UK DCDC, 2019/2020); SMART CITY TECHNOLOGIES. SMART BASE NEEDS. (US DoD-adjacent); Brayne (2018, megacity intelligence frameworks); Barcik et al. (2023, "Military Usage" section); Zavorka & Paar (2022); Sinergia (2018)
- Analytic note: this literature is predominantly engineering/defence-studies, methodologically theoretical or case-study, and confirms the *feasibility* claim of the framework from the side of the practitioner. It treats interoperability standards (MQTT, NGVA, MIP) as the transfer mechanism — the material substrate of "frictionless" transfer.

### T1-B · The military boomerang: war-zone tech returns to domestic policing (military→civil, critical) — ~16 records
The critical-geography canon on Graham's "boomerang effect": counterinsurgency and military surveillance technologies repatriated into civilian urban policing.
- Exemplars: Wiig (2018, Camden — Iraq/Afghanistan counterinsurgency tactics → Camden Metro PD); Wall (2011; 2013, "green-to-blue pipeline", explicitly uses "dual-use scopic technologies"); Coaffee (2015, ANPR "developed from former military technology"); Kitchen (2014, LRAD from Somali pirates/Iraq → G20 Toronto); Mendel (2015, "drone stare"); Schimmel (2021, NFL/Pentagon civic ritual)
- Policing-specific genealogy: The Public History and Private Future of Police Data (2021, PredPol funded by Army Research Office; vendors "demonstrate military applications to police to create demand"); Predict and Surveil (2021, Palantir counterinsurgency → LAPD); Sharma & Nijjar (2024, PredPol from Pentagon battlefield-casualty modelling); Arantes & Ribeiro (2021, Israel–Brazil security transfer); Dantas et al. (2017); Wernaart et al. (2023, drones); Lotfi-Jam (2025, military-entertainment complex)

### T1-C · Commercial data repurposed for state security (function creep; both directions) — ~12 records
The surveillance-capitalism/state-security nexus: data collected for marketing, retail, or civic purposes flowing into security apparatus.
- Exemplars: Amoore (2009 — the strongest Arrow-level account: association rules from Marks & Spencer/Wal-Mart → US VISIT, e-borders; "near-zero marginal cost", "oscillation" across spheres); Wilhelmsen (2022, commercial tracking → espionage, explicitly cites near-zero marginal cost); Leszczynski (2016, EMOTIVE tweets → security assemblage); Our Incriminating Lives (2023, Palantir fusing utility bills, foreclosure data, LexisNexis); Big Brother Watch (2019, Experian Mosaic → HART recidivism tool); Srivastava (2021, Google image recognition → drone targeting; data as "non-perishable, non-excludable"); Uliasz (2021, DukeMTMC: Army-funded civilian dataset → Chinese surveillance)
- Also: Social Media Monitoring (India); Tenney (2016); Bigo (2025, data brokers → border security); Josipović (2024, smartphone extraction from criminal prosecution → asylum); Kuldova (2022, compliance-industrial complex — military→corporate compliance)

### T1-D · Chinese smart-city exports and civil-military fusion (dual-use as geostrategy) — ~8 records
Dual-use framed as state strategy: "safe city" exports as vehicles of influence, surveillance, and military-civil fusion.
- Weber (2023, city brains → military command brains, Digihail dual contracts); Atha et al. (2020, USCC report — "civilian informatization policies… designed to augment China's military power"); Robinson (2020, "Serbia model" → Gulf); Huawei's Smart Cities and CCP Influence (2018); Smart Cities with Chinese Characteristics (2021); Sutter & Sayler (2023, LiDAR); Ziadah (2021, UAE — Western tech into Gulf authoritarianism); Paradigm Initiative (2025, Huawei in Uganda, Pegasus in Ghana)

### T1-E · Civilian infrastructure as weapon and target (physical dual-use) — ~5 records
A distinct, older modality: not software but pipes, wires, and housing repurposed as instruments of war.
- Salamanca (2011, Gaza — "infrastructural violence"); Coward (2009, "dual use facilities" under Iraq sanctions); Zeitoun (2018, Basrah water — dual-use sanctions *impeding* civilian repair); Coyles (2017, Belfast housing as security instrument); Graham (2012, new military urbanism — bridges B and E)

### T1-F · Dual-use as regulatory category — ~5 records
Export controls, sanctions regimes, AI Act loopholes: the law's own dual-use concept, lagging the technology.
- Zeitoun (2018); EU's AI Act fails (2024, export loophole); Paradigm Initiative (2025, EU Dual-Use Regulation); Lavers (2024, COTS FPV drones — "technology is inherently dual-use"); Wicks et al. (2003)

### T1 cross-cutting finding
**The Arrow mechanism is empirically under-theorised.** Coders flagged "does not engage with the economic properties of information goods" on the large majority of records. Only Amoore (2009), Wilhelmsen (2022), and Srivastava (2021) substantively connect transfer to software's cost structure. The literature documents *that* transfer happens, extensively, in both directions — but rarely theorises *why it is frictionless*. This is precisely the gap the dissertation's framework (Arrow × Deleuze × Michael) addresses.

Direction tally within T1 (explicit/tangential): civil→military dominates the technical cluster (A), military→civil dominates the critical cluster (B), "both" clusters in C/D.

## Theme 2 — Epistemic authority & black-boxing (n=117 scoring ≥2; 16 scoring 3)

### T2-A · The epistemology of predictive policing — ~22 records
The densest cluster: what a "pattern" is, who vouches for it, and what it hides.
- Exemplars: Kaufmann (2019 — "patterns are the epistemological core of predictive policing"; "the clean surface of the pattern makes it impossible to defend oneself against their results"); The Making of Crime Predictions (2021, Rio CrimeRadar — political choices "naturalized and de-politicized" in translation to code); Hälterlein (2021, three epistemologies); Chan (2016, correlation vs causation); Castro-Toledo (2023); Gatti (2022, genealogy from criminal cartography); Meijer (2019, no evidence base)
- Ethnographic counter-evidence: Buscariolli (2023, officers bridge "data holes" with ethnomethods); Barreneche (2019, Bogotá — institutional cultures "corrupt" crime data at input); Police Pushback (2017, officers dismiss algorithms as "witchcraft"/"voodoo science"); Alvear Moreno (2025, Santiago — officers negotiate colonial scripts)
- Review/critique: Tulumello (2018, 2022, Memphis Blue CRUSH); McElreath (2022); Das Polizieren der Zukunft (2015); Nascimento et al. (2025); Ugwudike (2022, AI audits)

### T2-B · Opacity by design: trade secrecy, procurement, and the law — ~15 records
Opacity is not (only) a technical property of ML; it is *institutionally manufactured* through proprietary procurement, national-security carve-outs, and evidentiary asymmetry.
- Exemplars: Erdogan (2024 — LED/AI Act "fail to lift the 'veil of secrecy' surrounding national security"; individualist remedies vs population-level effects); EPIC amicus (2026 — ALPR decisions "cloaked in objectivity and hidden in a proprietary black box"); Myanmar CCTV (2022 — "opacity is normalised as a feature and not a bug"); Coding Inequality (2019 — trade secrecy); Predict and Surveil (2021 — vendors hiding behind trade secrecy, "techwashing")
- Regulatory pushback: Barkane & Buka (2024); Mobilio (2023); EU AI Act open letters (2023, 2024); Big Brother Watch (2019); Shepherd (2024, Clearview — "digital policy literacy"); Paradigm Initiative (2025); NITI Aayog critique (2018); Raghuvanshi et al. (2025)

### T2-C · Dataism and the constructed truth — ~20 records
The theoretical core: algorithmic outputs are not found but *made*; correlation's claim to supersede causation; "smartmentality".
- Exemplars: Bigo (2025 — a "magical aura of scientific knowledge" given to "dubious correlations"); Amoore (2009 — calculations "conceal political difficulty, even discrimination and violence, within an apparently neutral and glossy techno-science"); Srivastava (2021); Matzner (2016, performativity — suspects produced "parasitically" on other datafied subjectivities); Lake (2017, hyperindividualist ontology); Kitchin (2018, "smartmentality"); Aradau (2022, decision as mundane partition of feature space); Uliasz (2021, operative images — "a reality filled with blind spots, inaccurate objects, and biases")
- Also: Završnik & Badalič (2021); Big Data, Crime and Social Control (2018); Leszczynski (2016); Tenney (2016); Williamson (2015); Lotfi-Jam (2025, realtime epistemologies); Marvin & Luque-Ayala (2017); Arrigo et al. (2020); Foth et al. (2021); Cole & Tran (2022); Kraus (2026); Martini (2025)

### T2-D · Racialised and colonial knowledge production — ~14 records
The strongest normative current: race is not a bias contaminating neutral systems but constitutive of what these systems *are*; epistemic authority as colonial authority.
- Exemplars: Sharma & Nijjar (2024 — "Policing does not have a 'racist history'. Policing makes race… Algorithms cannot 'code out' race"); Ziadah (2021 — "digital epidermalization", UAE); Irungbam (2026 — shutdowns as "epistemicide" (Santos)); Massaro (2011 — "white spatial imaginary" makes racialisation "politically impregnable")
- Occupation epistemologies: Tawil-Souri (2011 — "uncertainty becomes the ultimate system of control" at Qalandia); Shalhoub-Kevorkian (2017 — "uneven distribution of visual rights"); Bishara (2015, subaltern knowledge of the road); Grinberg (2016, children "puncture" security's "real fantasies")
- Also: Alvear Moreno (2025, coloniality of STAD in Santiago); Kasapoglu & Masso (2021, refugees as at-risk/risky); Gundhus (2021, migrants as threats); Caged by Data (2023, Zach Blas face cages); Fregonese (2024, "suspect communities")

### T2-E · Smart-city governance and the democratic deficit — ~20 records
Beyond policing: datafied urban governance bypassing deliberation, and counter-movements.
- Exemplars: Lynch (2020, Barcelona technological sovereignty); Waisová (2022, Egypt's New Administrative Capital as ordering instrument); Durr (2023, Miskolc — illiberal securitisation *overtly* political, complicating post-political accounts); Monroe (2017, Beirut TMC Twitter — citizens invert the "God's Eye View"); Bartmanski et al. (2023, Songdo — "seeing like a state" × "seeing like a corporation"); Pijpers (2025, Rotterdam waste oligopticons — fines "impossible to contest")
- Also: Figueiredo & Agyin (2019); Ip et al. (2020); Jacobs et al. (2024); Public Comment, Tracy (2023); McDuie-Ra & Lai (2019); McDuie-Ra (2024); Weber (2023); Atha et al. (2020); Making space with data (2020, Danish "ghettos" performed by statistics); Taylor (2021, living-lab ethics); Arcila (2022); Smart Cities as "Big Brother Only to the Dialogue Masses" (2022); Graham (2008, Robowar — "Combat Zones That See"); Smith (2022, Turkish Kurdistan)

### T2 cross-cutting findings
1. **Opacity is institutional before it is technical.** Across T2-B and the ethnographic records, the black box is produced by procurement law, trade secrecy, and security carve-outs — "transparency" fixes that ignore this (XAI, audits) recur as necessary-but-insufficient.
2. **The "bias" framing is contested from within the corpus.** The strongest T2-D records argue race/coloniality is constitutive, not contaminating — a direct dialogue with Michael's epistemic-authority concept: authority to define truth is authority over *whom* truth targets.
3. **The vertical/aerial gaze is a recurring epistemic figure** — God's Eye View (Monroe; Woods et al.), "cosmic view"/drone stare (Wall 2011), "verticalized omniscience" (Vertical Security 2010), Graham's (2012) machinic "Id-ing" replacing social identification. This is the hinge between T2 and T4: the epistemology that makes cities targetable.

## Theme 3 — City as programmable system / modulation (n=102 scoring ≥2; 10 scoring 3)

### T3-A · The programmable city as governance concept — ~11 records
The theory core: the city reconceived as operating system, platform, and calculable field.
- Exemplars: Marvin & Luque-Ayala (2017 — Urban OS "does not function to represent… but rather constructs a real that is to come" (Deleuze & Guattari); military/corporate rationality transfer); Lotfi-Jam (2025 — "realtime" as "shared operational condition" of military, entertainment, and urban systems); Graham (2012 — explicitly deploys Deleuze's societies of control); Kitchin (2018, "smartmentality"); Tenney (2016, "algorithmic-regulation", "automated democracy"); Williamson (2015, "programmable environments"); Leszczynski (2016, "cities are constituted by their data shadows"); Uliasz (2021); Foth et al. (2021); Arcila (2022); Big Data, Crime and Social Control (2018, "framelessness… total information capture")

### T3-B · Modulation beyond screens: atmosphere, behaviour, environment — ~7 records
A distinctive sub-current: control exercised through light, sound, smell, time, and affect rather than dashboards — the city as scripted environment.
- Exemplars: Schuilenburg & Peeters (2018 — "scripted architecture", pastoral power, inclusion via "positive triggers"); Smart Cities as "Big Brother Only to the Dialogue Masses" (2022 — Stratumseind; Foucault's security: keeping behaviour "around an average"; public space → consumption space); Fregonese (2024, "atmospheric fortification", "atmotechnics"); Baumann (2019 — time as infrastructure of control in East Jerusalem: deregulation + synchronisation); Shalhoub-Kevorkian (2017, "continuous sensory invasion"); Coaffee (2009, aesthetics of urban defences)

### T3-C · Living labs and test-bed urbanism — ~5 records
The city as experiment: populations enrolled as unwitting test subjects.
- Jacobs et al. (2024, Scheveningen); Taylor (2021, "exploitation as innovation" — living labs need research-ethics governance); Figueiredo & Agyin (2019, Eindhoven's hidden sensors); We Sense Trouble (2020, Roermond as living lab); Stratumseind (T3-B)

### T3-D · The battlespace version: military reads the city as system-of-systems — ~7 records
The hinge cluster for the dissertation: military doctrine independently arrives at the *same ontology* of the programmable city as smart-city boosterism.
- Exemplars: Graham (2008, Robowar — DARPA's Combat Zones That See: build "fully representative data profiles on the 'normal' time-space movement patterns of entire subject cities" to algorithmically "determine what is normal and what is not"); Danielsson (2025 — the "military urban" as system-of-systems, "living organism"); Brayne (2018, megacity intelligence frameworks); Atha et al. (2020) and Weber (2023) — the "city brain" as OODA loop, "world digital brain"; Riberto et al. (2018); Huawei's Smart Cities (2018)

### T3-E · Pre-crime modulation: the control-society policing literature — ~18 records
Deleuze's discipline→control shift operationalised as predictive policing, risk scoring, and preemption.
- Exemplars: Arrigo et al. (2020 — "pre-crime society of Deleuzian control"); Kuldova (2022, pre-crime compliance OS); Our Incriminating Lives (2023 — shift from query-based to *alert-based* continuous monitoring); Directed Surveillance; Coding Inequality (2019); Sharma & Nijjar (2024); Das Polizieren der Zukunft (2015); Gundhus (2021 — "potential dangerousness exists everywhere"); Kasapoglu & Masso (2021); Aradau (2022); Tulumello (2018, 2022); Alvear Moreno (2025); Kaufmann (2019); Hälterlein (2021); Alkhazraji (2024); POLICIAMENTO PREDITIVO (2019); EPIC (2024)

### T3-F · Analogue modulation: the administered and occupied city — ~16 records
A corrective to presentism: bureaucratic, architectural, and infrastructural systems modulated urban populations long before — and continuing alongside — software.
- Exemplars: Coyles (2017 — Belfast housing as "grid of intelligibility"; "controlled allocation" of tenancies as pre-emptive spatial design); Crane (2017 — SAU "remapped the capital into militarized zones"); Salamanca (2011 — Gaza's electricity/fuel/water switched on/off as "elastic" humanitarian modulation); Smith (2022 — Turkish Kurdistan: "insurgent-proof" Haussmannisation, "residential blocks set in surveillable space"); Goodfellow (2013 — Kigali's "engineering secure urbanisation"); Matrix of Control (2023, Palestine); Coward (2009, network-centric battlespace); Pallister-Wilkins (2016 — barriers as *devices of circulation*, not blockade: "the data it captured remains in use… stored on servers in Virginia")
- Also: Kienscherf (2011); Kitchen (2014, G20); Coaffee (2015, Olympic carceral); Massaro (2011, 2015); Schimmel (2021); Super Bowl framing (2011); Mendes (2015); Alsaid (2021); Shapiro (2019)

### T3-G · Contestation and alternatives — ~4 records
Artyushina (2020, Sidewalk's Urban Data Trust as assetisation); Kraus (2026, data justice); Foth et al. (2021, technological sovereignty); Lynch (2020, Barcelona — via T2-E)

### T3 cross-cutting findings
1. **Deleuze travels.** Explicit control-society engagement appears in both corpora across policing, smart-city, and occupation cases (Graham 2012; Arrigo; Pijpers; Irungbam; Tan; Coaffee 2009, 2015) — the framework's second pillar is well-grounded in the empirical literature.
2. **Modulation is multi-channel.** Screens and dashboards are one channel among atmosphere, environment, time, infrastructure switching, and tenancy allocation (T3-B, T3-F). The algorithmic city *intensifies and automates* an older administrative modulation — a direct answer to any "hype" objection.
3. **The military converges on the same ontology** (T3-D): doctrine treats the city as a programmable system-of-systems whose "normal" can be profiled — the precondition for targeting. This is where T3 hands off to T4.

## Theme 4 — Spatial destruction / domicide (n=76 scoring ≥2; 34 scoring 3 — highest intensity of all themes)

### T4-A · The urbicide canon: concept formation — ~8 records
- Exemplars: Graham (2004, "Postmortem city" — Hewitt: war as "the most thorough-going… occasion of collective violence that destroys places"; the Rizer concession: targeting "dual-use" electrical infrastructure in Iraq 1991 → 100,000 civilian deaths); Graham (2012 — urbicide's tripartite definition: cosmopolitan mixing / means of modern urban living / erasure of the "unmodern"); Coward (2009); Ḥanafī (2012, "spacio-cide" — "the weapons of mass destruction are not so much tanks as bulldozers"); Hourani (2024, "post-conflict-catastrophe complex"); Mendel (2015); Pasquetti (2019); Atkinson (2017, "autotomic space" — the city shedding a limb)
- Note: concept proliferation (urbicide, domicide, spacio-cide, place annihilation, slow urbicide, warchitecture, autotomic space, Dracula urbanism) signals an active, unsettled field.

### T4-B · Palestine as laboratory — ~25 records
The corpus's gravitational centre. Palestine functions as the paradigmatic case where every modality co-occurs.
- Concept frames: Cook (2008 — confined spaces as "laboratories where experiments to encourage Palestinian despair… are being refined"); Ḥanafī (2012); Golańska (2022, "slow urbicide" — bureaucratic neglect, pollution, vernacular obliteration as attritional violence); Benguita (2025, three records — Gaza 2023–25 as "Spatial Nakba": "Gaza is not merely being bombed—it is being unmade as a space of collective identity and political potential")
- Modalities: home demolition (Meade 2011; Jadallah 2014; Paz-Fuchs 2010 — planning as "anti-planning"); walking through walls (Bleibleh 2015; The atrocities of war 2023); walls/checkpoints/closure (Tawil-Souri 2011; Handelman 2010; Ball 2014; Harker 2009; Handel 2014; Peteet 2015 — Weisglass: "put the Palestinians on a diet, but not to make them die of hunger"); settlements/territorialisation (Volinz 2019; Cohen 2018, biospatial politics; Busbridge 2013); sensory/symbolic violence (Shalhoub-Kevorkian 2017; Pullan 2013; Vadasaria 2015, "necronationalism")
- Gaza 2023–25 documentation: Holail et al. (2024 — time-series satellite remote sensing quantifying escalating damage); Benguita (2025)

### T4-C · Kurdish Turkey: urbicide by razing *and* renewal — ~4 records
Smith (2022, two records — "Leviathan's Architect": tabula rasa reconstruction as continuation of war; "vernacular and communal architecture… replaced with housing units… that integrate them into state agencies and mortgage regimes"); Genç (2021 — "displaceability" produced by renewal *before* the warfare); Sen (2024 — nationalist infrastructure as symbolic cleansing); Çaylı (2016 — state media exteriorising the operation zone)

### T4-D · Domicide by planning: the peacetime continuum — ~10 records
Destruction without declared war: evictions, clearances, "renewal", and mega-event securitisation as home unmaking.
- Exemplars: Wilson & Wyly (2026, "Dracula urbanism" — "real-estate state kill-offs… banal eviscerating becomes an unexceptional thing"); Arantes & Ribeiro (2021, Rio's "Museum of Evictions"); Samara (2010, Cape Town); Goodfellow (2013, Kigali); Durr (2023, Miskolc); Coaffee (2015, Olympic "spatial cleansing"); Coyles (2017, Belfast); Massaro (2015, Philadelphia — including the MOVE bombing of a city block); Atkinson (2017); Scranton (2007, Baghdad); McGuire (2013)

### T4-E · Infrastructural warfare: targeting life-support — ~6 records
Salamanca (2011, Gaza — "infrastructural violence" as "hostile topography"); Zeitoun (2018, Basrah water); Coward (2009); Graham (2004, de-electrification of Iraq); Benguita (2025 — hospitals, schools, universities); Larrinaga (2016 — tear gas as "atmosterror": making "an unlivable milieu" (Sloterdijk))

### T4-F · Vertical sovereignty and the view from above — ~6 records
Wall (2011, 2013); Vertical Security in the Megacity (2010, police helicopter's "machinic-prosthetic view"); Graham (2008); Woods et al. (2024, volumetric); Hourani (2024). Weizman's politics of verticality is the most-cited concept in the theme — the T2 "God's Eye View" literalised.

### T4-G · Algorithmic targeting: the T1→T4 junction — ~5 records
The cluster the dissertation exists to expand — currently thin and very recent.
- Hourani (2024 — Lavender and Where's Daddy?: kill-list generation, targets "tracked to their homes… killed along with their families"; "gives lie to technophilic dreams of the sanitary smart bomb"); Lotfi-Jam (2025, AI-assisted kill chains); Završnik & Badalič (2021, Project Maven); Graham (2008, CTS as precursor); Holail et al. (2024 — the same sensing stack used to *document* destruction)

### T4-H · Resistance, counter-forensics, counter-sovereignty — ~8 records
Gazit (2014, counter-sovereignties); Shapiro (2019, forensic architecture as counter-forensics); Hallward (2008, Machsom Watch); Smith (2022, memory projects); Sen (2024); Bleibleh (2015, "making-do"); Grinberg (2016); Çaylı (2016)

### T4 cross-cutting findings
1. **"Dual-use" is the justification *inside* the destruction.** Benguita's coding surfaces the direct discursive link: "Hospitals, schools, mosques, media institutions, and universities have been struck under the rationale of dual-use or symbolic alignment with resistance." The legal-military dual-use category (T1-F) is the warrant for urbicide (T4) — the dissertation's thesis confirmed *within* the corpus's own vocabulary.
2. **Destruction is a cycle, not an event.** Renewal precedes warfare (Genç; Coyles) and follows it as "tabula rasa" continuation (Smith; Hourani's catastrophe complex). Wilson & Wyly extend the continuum into peacetime smart-city development — domicide by planning is the same logic at lower intensity.
3. **Palestine's paradigmatic weight is itself a finding** — and a scope consideration: the field's empirical base is heavily concentrated; Kurdish Turkey, Belfast, Rio, Kigali, Cape Town, Baghdad provide the comparative spread.
4. **The algorithmic-urbicide literature is nascent** (T4-G: five records, all 2021+). The gap between an established urbicide canon (T4-A/B) and an established algorithmic-governance literature (T2/T3) is exactly the dissertation's research-agenda space.

## Cross-theme synthesis (for Discussion chapter)

The four themes form a causal chain, and the corpus maps it with varying density:
- **T1 (mechanism): 86** — transfer happens, both directions, but the *why* (Arrow) is under-theorised → framework contribution #1
- **T2 (authority): 117** — opacity is institutionally manufactured; algorithmic outputs granted constructed truth → framework contribution #2 (Michael extension into urban governance)
- **T3 (ontology): 102** — city read as programmable system by boosters (A), critics (E), and the military (D) alike → framework contribution #3 (De Landa/Deleuze validated across adversarial literatures)
- **T4 (consequence): 76** — the most theoretically intense (34 records at score 3) but the thinnest on the *algorithmic* mode of destruction (T4-G: 5 records)

**The corpus's own structure performs the thesis:** keyword search found the governance side (main corpus: T1 1.13, T4 0.53); citation network found the destruction side (snowball: T1 0.59, T4 1.33). The literatures barely talk to each other — T4-G's five records are nearly the only bridges. The "Search B asymmetry" flagged at scoping is reproduced at corpus scale.
